## Architectural Visualization Methods 1

**ARC 3133 · Florida Atlantic University · School of Architecture**

A course on computational design thinking for architectural representation — algorithmic methods, parametric principles, and the fabrication of what they produce.

## Three paradigms

The course is structured around three ways of designing with code, each a strict extension of the one before it.

| | Paradigm | What it means | Output is | Where |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | **Procedural** | Form as a fixed sequence of operations. You define the steps. | Expected | Module 0 |
| 02 | **Parametric** | Relationships between inputs and form. You define the rules of variation. | A family — you know the range, not each member | Modules 1–3 |
| 03 | **Generative** | A rule applied to its own output, repeatedly. You define the rule and the starting state. | Unknown, within a spectrum you set | Final |

A procedure is a fixed recipe. A parametric system is a recipe with dials. A generative system is a recipe that reads its own output and decides what to do next.

> **The designer is no longer making choices about a single, final object, but creating a matrix encompassing an entire population of possible designs.**

## Modules

| Module | Subject | Fabrication | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — Procedural** | Form as an ordered sequence of operations, diagrammed and written as executable instructions | Additive, introductory | Small print |
| **1 — Fields** | Lists, arrays and attractors — organizing and differentiating collections | Plotter demonstration | — |
| **2 — Section** | Contours, sequence and filtering — cutting continuous geometry into ordered parts | Computer-controlled cutting | Sectional assembly |
| **3 — Tessellation** | Modularity, grids and mapping — one component, varied across a surface | Additive manufacturing | 3D-printed vault or façade |
| **Final — Generative** | A tool that iterates: growth, agents, cellular automata | — | — |

## How it is taught

**Students build systems as graphs and learn to read code rather than write it from scratch.** Visual programming — Grasshopper and Blender Geometry Nodes — makes data flow visible: you can see where values come from, what transforms them, and where they go.

Code is not absent. It is approached as something to read, trace and modify. The Final closes the loop: students take a graph they have built, see the same logic written as text, and extend it into something a graph cannot easily express.

**The point of learning to read code is that you can then direct, verify and correct a tool that writes it.**

## Class format

Every class runs the same way:

1. **Before class** — watch the tutorial. All tutorials are pre-recorded and released the week before, in both Grasshopper and Geometry Nodes versions.
2. **Questions and troubleshooting** — the first part of class is spent on what did not work. Bring the file, not the description.
3. **Assignment briefing** — requirements and precedents.
4. **Supervised work and critique.**

## Making

Every module has a physical counterpart to its computational subject. Fabrication runs through two modes of computer-controlled making — cutting and additive manufacturing — introduced at object scale in Module 0 and at system scale in Modules 2 and 3.
