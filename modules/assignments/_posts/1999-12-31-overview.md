## Meeting the stated minimums earns a C

Every assignment lists requirements that can be counted. Counting them is the floor, not the target. A submission that satisfies every numerical requirement and produces a weak image is a passing submission, not a good one.

The difference between a C and an A is visual judgement, and it is not reducible to a checklist.

## Every A-assignment carries a precedent analysis

Two or three sentences on a reference image: what varies, what is held constant, what was chosen and what was computed.

## Prohibited shortcuts

A tool that automates the concept an assignment exists to teach may not be used in that assignment.

| Assignment | Prohibited |
|---|---|
| All sheets, 0.2, Booklet | Canva, Adobe Express, template-driven layout apps, PowerPoint, Google Slides |
| 0.1 | Imported or downloaded models. Procedures may not reference software commands |
| 1.1 | Array add-ons, scatter and distribution tools, preset attractor falloff plugins |
| 2.1 / 2.2 | Automated contour and sectioning plugins; one-click waffle and slotting generators |
| 3.1 / 3.2 | Panelization libraries are **permitted** for generating the tessellation. Their preset gradient, attractor and variation components are not |
| All Grasshopper work | Cross Reference may be used **once** per definition |

**How this is checked:** not by auditing files, but by asking. Each submission includes a short written statement of the mechanism it uses, in your own words. At pin-up you may be asked to explain or alter that mechanism.
