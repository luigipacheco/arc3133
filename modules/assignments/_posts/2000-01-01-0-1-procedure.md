**Due Class 4 · Sep 15 · 8%** · Module 0 — Procedural

Select an architectural object — a building, a pavilion, a fragment — and reconstruct it as a **procedure**: a sequence of primitive solids and Boolean operations that produces its massing. Then document that procedure two ways.

## Deliverable 1 — The process diagram

A sequential axonometric set in the manner of BIG's massing diagrams: one drawing per operation, so the transformation of the geometry can be read step by step, plus one larger final isometric with human scale, context, shadows and annotation.

The diagram is the visual argument.

## Deliverable 2 — The procedure

The same construction written as **pseudocode** — a formal notation another person could execute without seeing your model.

```
PRIMITIVES          TRANSFORMS               BOOLEANS
box(x, y, z)        move(s, x, y, z)         union(a, b)
cylinder(r, h)      rotate(s, axis, deg)     subtract(a, b)
sphere(r)           scale(s, factor)         intersect(a, b)
cone(r, h)
```

Three rules govern it:

- Every shape is created **centred on the origin (0, 0, 0)**. Position is expressed as a move from there.
- **Units are abstract** — `3` units, not 3 metres. The procedure is scale-independent.
- **Every step assigns a name**, so the order of an operation is never in doubt.

No screenshots, no software commands. `subtract(mass, void)`, never *"boolean difference with Cylinder.003"*.

LeWitt wrote in plain English, and different executors produced different drawings; that ambiguity was his subject. Yours must produce **one** result, so the notation is tightened.

Both deliverables go on the same 17" × 11" sheet.

## The test is execution

At **Class 5**, procedures are exchanged and each student builds a classmate's object from the written procedure alone. What the classmate produces is the assessment.

Formal notation removes most ambiguity. What remains — which axis, which reference point, whether a move applies to the centre — is exactly what the exchange exposes, and is the finding.

## Requirements

- 5+ Boolean operations
- 4+ distinct primitive types
- 2+ translations, 1+ rotation
- Procedure in the notation above — one operation per line, every step named
- Operation history intact and demonstrable
- All primitives generated in-session; no imported models
- One precedent analysis — 2–3 sentences on why the massing reads as a sequence

## First print — required

Your CSG result is 3D printed at small scale and presented at the **Midterm, Class 8**.

- **60 mm** maximum bounding box, draft layer height
- **No supports permitted** — orientation is your only lever
- An orientation diagram on the sheet: chosen orientation, the overhangs it produces, why you chose it over an alternative
- If the geometry cannot print unsupported in any orientation, revise the geometry. That is a legitimate and expected outcome

Assessed on printability and on your reasoning. Craft and finish belong to 3.2.

**No credit is issued for 0.1 without the print.**

## Assessed on

Clarity and completeness of the procedure, whether it can actually be executed, quality of the axonometric set, and graphic execution of the final isometric. **The diagram and the procedure carry equal weight** — a beautiful diagram with a vague procedure fails half the assignment.
