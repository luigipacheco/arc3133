Nothing here is required. Each is listed against the point in the semester where it earns its place.

## Computational design — how these systems work

**Form+Code in Design, Art, and Architecture** — Casey Reas, Chandler McWilliams, LUST · Princeton Architectural Press, 2010 · ISBN 978-1568989372
The closest book to this course's structure. Organized by *what code does* — repeat, transform, parameterize, visualize, simulate — rather than by software. Read alongside Modules 1–3.

**The Nature of Code** — Daniel Shiffman · free at [natureofcode.com](https://natureofcode.com/)
**The reference for the Final.** Vectors and forces, agents and steering behaviours, cellular automata, fractals and L-systems, genetic algorithms. Code is p5.js, but the logic reads directly and the explanations are unusually clear.

**Elements of Parametric Design** — Robert Woodbury · Routledge, 2010 · ISBN 978-0415779876
The theory behind why parametric systems behave as they do — propagation, dependency, and the cost of change.

**AAD Algorithms-Aided Design** — Arturo Tedeschi · Le Penseur · ISBN 978-8895315300
Practical Grasshopper reference.

**Advanced 3D Printing with Grasshopper®: Clay and FDM** — García Cuevas + Pugliese · 2020 · ISBN 9798635379011
Module 3.2, and for anyone wanting to control the toolpath rather than hand geometry to a slicer. The letter test in Class 3 comes from here.

## Foundational

**Point and Line to Plane** — Wassily Kandinsky · Dover · ISBN 978-0486238081
Historical grounding rather than method.

**Paragraphs on Conceptual Art** — Sol LeWitt (1967), and the *Wall Drawings*
Module 0 — the instruction as the work.

**Learnable Programming** — Bret Victor · [worrydream.com](http://worrydream.com/LearnableProgramming/)
Why a system you can see changing is easier to understand than one you cannot.
