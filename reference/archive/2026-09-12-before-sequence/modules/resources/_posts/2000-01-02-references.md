## Contemporary practitioners

A starting set. These are working practitioners publishing continuously — treat them as a live feed rather than a fixed bibliography. The point is to see what a high standard looks like right now, not to imitate a specific image.

| Reference | Relevant to | Why |
|---|---|---|
| [@mmnksr](https://www.instagram.com/mmnksr/) | Modules 1–2 | Point clouds and sections — density and depth built from discrete elements |
| [@mantissa.xyz](https://www.instagram.com/mantissa.xyz/) | Modules 1–2 | The same territory at a different resolution; useful read against @mmnksr |
| [@saul_kim_](https://www.instagram.com/saul_kim_/) | All modules | Minimalist visualization — restraint, line weight, and how much can be removed before an image stops reading |
| [@facadepot](https://www.instagram.com/facadepot/) | Module 3 | Modular façades — component logic and variation across a surface |

Two of these cover the same subject deliberately. Comparing how @mmnksr and @mantissa.xyz each resolve a point cloud is more instructive than studying either alone: the technique is shared, the graphic decisions are not, and the difference between them is exactly what this course assesses.

## Three questions for every precedent

1. What is the module?
2. What generates the positions?
3. What varies across the field, and what drives it?

And one more, for attractor work: **where is the attractor?** Sometimes it is a door, a view, a structural point, the sun. Sometimes it is nowhere — chosen purely for the image. Both are legitimate. You should know which you are doing.
